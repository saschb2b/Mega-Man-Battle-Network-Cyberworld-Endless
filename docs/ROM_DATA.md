# ROM data

All offsets are for Mega Man Battle Network 6: Cybeast Gregar (USA), SHA-1
`89fe0bac4fd3d2ab1d2ca35e87ef8b1294a84cd6`, and live in `RomLayout` in
`src/core/rom.c`. The [bn6f disassembly](https://github.com/dism-exe/bn6f)
targets Cybeast Falzar; most code-region tables sit at the same addresses in
Gregar, and the rest were found by searching the Gregar ROM for the
structures the disassembly describes. `tools/romlab` runs the ROM in libmgba
to confirm findings against real frames and VRAM.

| Sprite pointer lists | `0x031CC4` | Ten pointers, the first pointing just past the list (`SpritePointersList`). Every category renders correctly. |
| Chip records (0x2C bytes) | `0x021DA8` | Same address as Falzar's `ChipDataArr`. Cannon = 40 power, codes A B C *. Codes at 0, rarity 0-4 at 5, library type at 7 (0 standard, 1 Mega, 2 Giga, 3 secret, 4 Program Advance), library number at 0x15, library flags at 0x16 (bit 0: the other version's Navi chips and Otenko; 0x30: unused arm and dark chips; 0x10 alone: the Beast chips). A run can find or buy 252 of them (`src/core/chip_pool.c`): standard 1-202, the Megas 221-277 but Falzar's Navis, the ten Gigas 301-310. Records 203-220 are blank, 315 on pseudo-chips. |
| Chip names | `0x6E88D0`, `0x6E92D8` | Text archives found by encoding "Cannon" with the game's character table; pointers in code reference both. |
| Enemy id table | `0x0182C4` | `GetVerActorTyAndAIIdx`: (version, actor type, AI index) triples. |
| Font | `0x6B5A2C` | 8x16 glyphs indexed by character code (the battle font); the run summary uses it. |
| Title screen | `RomLayout.title`; load table at `0x02FCD8` | The game's own load list (source, VRAM or palette-buffer destination, size) gives the LZ77 blocks: the 256-colour picture's tiles `0x7F3040` (to 0x06000000), PRESS START / NEW GAME / CONTINUE `0x7F1EBC` (OBJ tile 1 on) with palettes `0x7F216C` and `0x7F218C`, and the copyright line `0x7F21EC` (8 OBJs of 32x32, palette `0x7F2C20`). The picture's 32x20 map sits at `0x7F7CFC`, its palette at `0x7F2E40` (banks 0-13). The picture is one layer, told apart by palette bank: banks 0-4 draw the logo, 5-6 the blue behind it and 7-13 the Cybeast (the six palette scripts at `0x02F5F0` pulse banks 7-10 and 13-14, the Cybeast only). The logo's pixels split into the emblem, MEGAMAN and BATTLE NETWORK (rows up to 95), the CYBEAST GREGAR plate (rows 97-112, not connected) and the 6, which touches the N and the K; the engine cuts the 6 along its black outline (x 177 on row 44 to 162 on row 81, 175 from BATTLE NETWORK down) and its dark rim (indices 0x01-0x02, 0x16-0x1C). Check by drawing indices 1-0x4F of the picture with that cut: no 6 pixels remain and the N keeps its outline. The menu arrow is 3 frames at `0x6A280C` (palette `0x6A344C`). Positions and timing came from recorded OAM and palette RAM: PRESS START blinks 32/32 and START plays sound 0x67; NEW GAME plays 0x9D, CONTINUE 0x9C, and the music stops. |
| Boot and warps (`src/emu/boot.c`) | `0x02009CD0` eChatbox, `0x02009F38` its flags, `0x02011BB0` Warp2011bb0, `0x02011C50` CutsceneState, `0x08005C05` map_triggerEnterMapOnWarp, `0x080050EC` cbGameState | The engine's warp copies a warp record into Warp2011bb0 and calls the game's own warp routine from a stub in the free ROM space, borrowing cbGameState (run every frame of the game mode) for one frame; the overworld routine at `0x08005A8C` it borrowed before does not run on every map frame. After NEW GAME it warps to Seaside Area 1 and ends the intro by clearing the cutscene script and closing the chat box as the chat script's end command (E6) does. Found in the bn6f disassembly and tested in `tools/romlab`. |
| Encounter roll (`src/director/encounter.c`) | `0x0ABD30` | Returns the `BattleSettings` record (bn6f `battle_settings_struct`, 16 bytes: field, music, type, background, side modifier, options, entity list pointer) for a random battle. The engine moves its first 12 bytes (position independent) into a trampoline and wraps it, so the game's own step counter and chance still decide when a battle happens and only a non-NULL result is swapped for the engine's record in the free ROM space. Entity entries are kind (0x00 MegaMan, 0x11 enemy), panel (row << 4 \| column, from 1) and the enemy id, ending with 0xF0. |
| Random battles (`src/core/formations.c`) | `0x020170` four tables | The default table and three that event flags `0x67F`, `0x680` and `0x681` choose while story scenes set them (bn6f `sub_80AA5F4`); compared pointer by pointer they differ only in Central and Seaside. Each table holds two pointers, real world and internet; the internet one lists per map group (from 0x80) per map a run of 16-byte BattleSettings ending with 0xFF (bn6f `sub_80AA5F4`). Byte 0 is the battlefield (the panels' layout: grass, holes, ice, poison...), bytes 12-15 the entity list. Byte 7 says when a battle may come: 0 always, 1 a rare battle (the game waits for EVENT_1D8 and 16 or 32 of the family deleted, counted at `0x02000070`; the engine leaves these out), 3-11 an SP navi each behind one of EVENT_212-219 and 222 (BlastMan, DiveMan, CircusMan, JudgeMan, ElementMan, navi 17, Colonel, Bass BX, GBeast). Every internet map has 10-50 of them: Central's Mettaur, Swordy, Quaker, Champy, Trumpy, OldStov, Gunner, Armadill; Seaside's Piranha, Puffy, StarFish; Green's BombCorn, HonyBmbr, Armadill; Sky's ScarCrow, FgtrPlne, Catack; Undernet's BigHat, SnakeArm, PulsBulb, DarkMech, Shrubby, Cragger; Graveyard and Underground most of the 29 families, with ErthDrgn and Nghtmare; each area also hides a rare pair and one SP navi. An area fights the formations of its maps (`RomLayout.net_area[].battles`), weighted by how many tables list them. The virus names archive (`TextScriptVirusChipNames`) is indexed by the enemy id of `0x0182C4`. |
| Map takeover (`src/map/mapslot.c`) | `0x0347E0` NPCList_maps80, `0x034670` map scripts, `0x03483C` object spawners, `0x0A484C` Mystery Data groups, `0x02004348` Mystery Data picks | Per group (index group − 0x80; the internet is 0x90-0x96) the engine points the layer's map at its own NPC list, empty map scripts and objects, and its Mystery Data (12-byte entries: type 5 green or 1 blue, flag 0x1400+n, one placement `[1, 0x20, X, Y, Z]`, one content `[kind, 0x20, 0xFF, code, value]`; kinds 1 chip, 3 zenny, 4 key item, 5 BugFrags). The object table is the literal of the spawner's first `ldr r1, [pc]`. |
| Map sprite lists (`src/map/mapslot.c`) | `0x03093C` EnterMap_InternetMapGroupJumptable | Each group's loader passes a per-map list of `(0x1C, sprite)` pairs ending `0xFFFF` to uncompSprite; compressed overworld objects (pointer bit 31, like the Chip Trader 0x5C) only draw once listed. The table is the literal of the loader's `lsl r1, r1, #2; ldr r0, [pc]; ldr r0, [r0, r1]`; the engine replaces the map's list with the layer's own objects (the original's would fill the decompression buffer). |
| NPC and text scripts (`src/layer/npc.c`, `text.c`) | charmap and commands per bn6f `text_script_commands.inc` | NPCs are bn6f NPC bytecode (0x08 visible, 0x25 sprite `index, category * 4`, 0x14 position, 0x16 animation, 0x44 text script and archive, 0x10 pause, 0x02 jump). Layer dialogue is a text archive (u16 offsets, then scripts) using E8 open, E7 wait, F2 clear, E6 end, FC 03 13 full HP, FB 05 shop, FB 06 01 BugFrag trader, FB 06 03 Chip Trader. NPC command 0x13 keeps an object from turning to MegaMan when spoken to; objects have no such frames and would vanish. |
| Chip Traders (`src/layer/trader.c`) | `0x086C3758` TextScriptChipTrader, `0x0804CDE0` prize pools, `0x0809B7E4` trader kinds | The trader archive is stored uncompressed; script 0 is the Chip Trader (3 chips), 6 the Chip Trader Special (10), 12 the BugFrag Trader, each with the game's Yes/No, not-enough lines and "Try again?". The trade screen (`selectChipTraderRewardList`, Gregar `0x0804CDB4`) looks the current map (group << 8 \| number) up in five 12-byte entries (map key, chip list of 6-byte records ending with 0, weights per rarity): 0x104 70 chips of rarity 1-3, 0x501 130 of 1-4, 0x000 108 of 1-3, 0x400 163 mostly 3-4, 0x9301 60 of 3-5 for BugFrags. With no match it returns no list and draws garbage prizes. `0x0809B7BC` looks the map up in six 8-byte entries (map key, byte) for the trader's script, which picks the lines the screen shows; with no match it takes the map number. Each layer rewrites the first entry of both tables with its own map. Falzar's tables are at `0x0804C01C` and in `asm03_2.s`. |
| Floor sprites (`src/layer/npc.c`) | NPC commands `0x1B`, `0x0C` | Overworld sprites share MegaMan's layer priority, which coordinate data section 2 sets per position (`checkLayerPriority_80318b0`), and the game orders them in OAM front to back by depth. `ns_set_layer_priority_override_to_3` puts a pad at priority 3, under MegaMan and over the floor layers. That alone fails from behind: between sprites the GBA draws the earlier OAM entry whatever their priority bits, so a pad sorted in front of MegaMan took the pixels over his feet and then lost them to the floor layer. Pads therefore stand 64 units further back in depth (+X, -Y) and 64 lower, which draws them in the same place but sorts them after MegaMan, and `ns_shift_center` (x, y, z signed bytes, stored in the object's CenterOffsetX/Y/Z) moves their collision and talk centre back to the pad. |
| Shops (`src/layer/shop.c`) | `0x046B68` shop descriptors, `0x047D70` initial shop data, eToolkit+0x54 ShopDataPtr | A descriptor is 16 bytes: currency (0 zenny, 1 BugFrags, 2 Chip Order), keeper text, offset into the shop data, entries. Entries are 8 bytes: kind (1 item: 0x70 HPMemory, 0x80+ SubChips; 2 chip; 3 NaviCust program), stock (0xFF unlimited), u16 id, code (program color), 0, u16 price in hundreds. The shop screen lists only entries that match the ROM's initial data, so layers write their stock to both; shops 0 (the Net Dealer, keeper face 0x3E) and 3 (the NaviCust vendor, keeper face 0x42, zenny, 8 entries: descriptor `0x046B98`) are taken over. The layers' picks come from the initial data as the player's ROM holds it, never from the core's rewritten copy, so a layer's stock does not depend on the layers built before it. Traced in `tools/romlab`. |
| Key items (`src/director/director.c`) | eToolkit+0x50 KeyItemsPtr (`0x02003134`), checks at `0x020004E0` and eToolkit+0x78 | One count per key item id; `CheckKeyItem` returns 0 unless byte id of the first check XOR 0x55 equals byte id of the second, which the game sets when it gives the item. | Warp pads (`src/map/coords.c`, `mapslot.c`) | coordinate data section 3, warp lists per group (first `ldr r0, [pc]` literal of each EnterMapGroup, e.g. `0x08072410` for Central), `0x02011BB0` Warp2011bb0, event flags `0x16F0`+n | Section 3 has the walls' format; a pad is 3x3 cells with shape `[z, warp index, height, type]`, types 9-C at the corners and 0x11 elsewhere (Central Area 1's own pads). `0x080058D0` checks the trigger under MegaMan each frame on the map: index 1-15 unless flag `0x16F0`+index is set, then sets Warp2011bb0 +0x10 to 1 and +0x11 to the index and runs the entry's departure (byte 2 of the 16-byte WarpData: group, number, departure, facing, x, y, z; 8 is the jack-out cutscene). | Net maps (`src/map/area_src.c`, `netmap.c`) | `0x0329C4` map descriptors, `0x03354C` coordinate data, `RomLayout.net_area[]` | One pointer per map group (0x80 first) to 12-byte descriptors per map: a tile set (two headers of word count, offset to LZ77 data and VRAM offset), a palette (a 4-byte size, then 16 banks) and a tile map (width, height, 0, 0, u32 12, u32 offset of layer 2 in the decompressed data, then LZ77). Coordinate data is a 16-byte header of four section offsets and LZ77; section 0 lists wall cells (8x8 world units, key (y+127)*254+(x+127)) with 4-byte shapes (lowest z, flag, height, type 1-8). Each biome learns its tiles from one map (Central 0x90:0, Seaside 0x91:0, Sky 0x94:1, Green 0x92:0, Graveyard 0x96:1, Undernet 0x95:0, Undernet Zero 0x95:1, Underground 0x93:1, and from the computer and homepage groups a comp 0x8C:0, a homepage 0x88:3 and a comp 0x8C:1). Groups 0x80-0x85 are the story's comps (Robot Control 0x80, Aquarium 0x81, Judge Tree 0x82, Mr. Weather 0x83, Pavilion 0x85:0-3 in the other four's tiles, CopyBot 0x85:4); 0x88 holds Lan's (0), ACDC (1), Aquarium (3), Green (5) and Sky (6) homepages. The groups' descriptor tables run into each other (0x81's starts at 0x80's entry 2). Maps of a group share one tile set and palette, so an area also learns from its other maps (`net_area.more`), and each map at each floor height with nine panels or more (section 1 heights, type 0x11): a copy drawn that many pixels lower shows the raised floor where ground floor would be. CopyBot's comp (0x85:4) cannot be split by hue (its walkways' teal discs share the purple plateaus' bucket, their white panels the rims'), so it is learned by shape (`TILES_BY_SHAPE`): platform floor lies in 2x2 blocks with floor all around, walkways and rims are the other floor; its plateaus stand at height 32 on ornamental pods (faces of 43), reached by ladders (ramp cells, type 0x14) from walkways at 0; its tall spikes stand on the second tile layer in front of the plateaus' faces (their tips over the rims at the front corners), with its claws and ladders. Aquarium Comp's glass pads stand at height 16 on legs: stairs of ramp cells (types 0x13 and 0x14, rising 4 to 16 from its water at 0; section 1 of 0x81:2) lead up to them, drawn on the second tile layer, and its glass walkways meet their rims at 16; nothing at 0 joins a pad flush (`net_area.pads_apart`). The game shows a net map's second tile layer in front of its first, as in the real world: on every area's layers BG1CNT reads 0x1D03 (the first layer, priority 3) and BG2CNT 0x3E02 (the second, priority 2); read with `emu_read16` at 0x0400000A and 0x0400000C on a generated layer. Section 0 lists only walls (types 1-12); the walls ring every floor and again every hole in it, so a panel lies on floor when it is inside an odd number of rings. Battle backgrounds by id: 0x00 ACDC HP, 0x01 Green HP, 0x03 Aquarium HP, 0x04 sky, 0x05 Judge Tree, 0x06 comp grid, 0x07/0x09 Central, 0x08 lab, 0x0A Aquarium Comp, 0x0B Seaside, 0x0C/0x0D Green, 0x0E blue comp, 0x0F/0x11 Undernet, 0x10 Mr. Weather, 0x12/0x14 Graveyard, 0x13 Secret, 0x15 Nest; 0x02 is blank. A random battle's record leaves its background at 0xFF (the map's). Groups 0x8C and 0x8D (16 maps each) are the real world's computers, mostly two layouts copied; 0x88 holds homepages in four colour pairs. Their random battles give WindBox and most families; they play song 0x13, and the engine gives them battle backgrounds 0x06, 0x03 and 0x08. Of the 22 backgrounds, the surface areas and the Graveyard and Undernet alternate between two (Central 0x07/0x09, Seaside 0x0B/0x0A, Sky 0x04/0x10, Green 0x0D/0x0C, Graveyard 0x14/0x12, Undernet 0x0F/0x11). Overworld sprite list 6 has CircusMan at 0x54 and Colonel at 0x53 (navi 14 and 18): tiles are classed by their place in a 32x32 panel and the floor around it, keeping panels whose middle and four points around it are drawn (the computers' platforms hang legs into the gaps) and whose middle has the listed hue (`styles`, 12 buckets plus grey); walkways learn from the panels of a second hue (`walk_styles`: Central 6, Seaside 1-2, Sky 6 and 8, Green 0, Graveyard 8, Undernet 10-11). Undernet-style maps draw their static in the second tile layer, so only the first marks floor there and their second-layer entries are not copied (`bg_in_map`). Elsewhere the originals set on the second layer what stands in front of a floor: Seaside Area 1's boardwalks where they cross its blue platform, Central Area 2's bridge over its green floor, the red ornaments on Green's and Central's pads, CopyBot's spikes and claws, the corner of a platform where it overlaps another on screen; and some floors, the Judge Tree's panel middles (its first layer draws only their frames) and Seaside Areas 2 and 3's blue platforms. Checked by drawing each layer alone (`area_src_render` with the other layer cleared) and both as the game shows them; `tiles.c` learns a tile's first layer alone where it draws the floor, and its second too only where the floor needs it. Judge Tree Comp 3's courts (0x82:2, and 0x85:1 in the same tiles) are flat red panels in hue bucket 0, its brick panels bucket 1: they count as floor but their tiles are left out (`net_area.skip_styles`). The small platforms the Judge Tree's maps join by bridges are round tree stumps, Sky's round pods; a square pad takes neither look (`TILES_NO_PAD_LOOK`). Every map draws its floor 3-4 pixels below the panel edges its walls give, with side faces about 9 pixels tall under the bottom edges; the computers and homepages also hang a pedestal about 18 pixels deep under each panel's lower corner. `tiles.c` measures all three per map and tests each map's tiles with its own faces and legs (Robot Control's white platforms stand on 17-pixel faces, its circuit walkways on 5) but with the floor where the first map draws it: Sky's raised views measure their floor 0 pixels down, and their tiles left steps along the edges. A generated map also avoids setting side by side tiles no original map does (`seams.c`, `tilemap.c`), and tiles whose floor stops on a tile's own edge above or below an empty one. |
