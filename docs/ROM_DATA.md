# ROM data

All offsets are for Mega Man Battle Network 6: Cybeast Gregar (USA), SHA-1
`89fe0bac4fd3d2ab1d2ca35e87ef8b1294a84cd6`, and live in `RomLayout` in
`src/core/rom.c`. The [bn6f disassembly](https://github.com/dism-exe/bn6f)
targets Cybeast Falzar; most code-region tables sit at the same addresses in
Gregar, and the rest were found by searching the Gregar ROM for the
structures the disassembly describes. `tools/romlab` runs the ROM in libmgba
to confirm findings against real frames and VRAM.

| Chip records (0x2C bytes) | `0x021DA8` | Same address as Falzar's `ChipDataArr`. Cannon = 40 power, codes A B C *. Codes at 0, rarity 0-4 at 5, library type at 7 (0 standard, 1 Mega, 2 Giga, 3 secret, 4 Program Advance), attack family at 0xB (27 the Navi chips, 81 the Dragons, 73 MchnSwrd's line, 21 placed objects and traps, 36 TankCan1-3: chips 12-14, power 120, 160 and 200, which fire after a wind-up), lock-on setting at 0xF (1 for chips that strike an enemy outright, Cannon, the swords, bombs; 0 for traps, counters, MchnSwrd's paralysed-only cut, VarSwrd's key inputs, GolmHit's and TimeBom's delays, and the Navi chips and Dragons; checked against those chips), library number at 0x15, library flags at 0x16 (bit 0: the other version's Navi chips and Otenko; 0x30: the arm chips and the five DarkChips the US version cut, whose 0x20 keeps a chip out of the pack's list (BN6's own DarkChips, below); 0x10 alone: the Beast chips), and two picture pointers at 0x24 and 0x28, which the chips cut from the US version share as one blank (`0x086F3AF0`, the same word in both). A run can find or buy 249 of them (`src/core/chip_pool.c`): standard 1-202, the Megas 221-274 but Falzar's Navis, the ten Gigas 301-310. HackJack, HackJck EX and HackJck SP (275-277, Megas of a Navi the Japanese version has) pass every rule but the blank picture: taken from an official vault, one used in battle made MegaMan vanish (his Navi's sprite is a white dot in the US version, sprite category 0x3F) until he was deleted (issue #15). Verify by listing the records whose words at 0x24 and 0x28 are equal: 34, all left out by the other rules but 275-277. Records 203-220 are blank, 315 on pseudo-chips. |

| Title screen | `RomLayout.title`; load table at `0x02FCD8` | The game's own load list (source, VRAM or palette-buffer destination, size) gives the LZ77 blocks: the 256-colour picture's tiles `0x7F3040` (to 0x06000000), PRESS START / NEW GAME / CONTINUE `0x7F1EBC` (OBJ tile 1 on) with palettes `0x7F216C` and `0x7F218C`, and the copyright line `0x7F21EC` (8 OBJs of 32x32, palette `0x7F2C20`). The picture's 32x20 map sits at `0x7F7CFC`, its palette at `0x7F2E40` (banks 0-13). The picture is one layer, told apart by palette bank: banks 0-4 draw the logo, 5-6 the blue behind it and 7-13 the Cybeast (the six palette scripts at `0x02F5F0` pulse banks 7-10 and 13-14, the Cybeast only). | BN6's wait for a frame (`src/emu/idle.c`, issue #35) | `0x080003A0` main_awaitFrame, its loop at `0x080003A6`; DISPSTAT `0x04000004`, IE `0x04000200`, IME `0x04000208` | bn6f `main_awaitFrame`, at the same address in Gregar: `ldr r0,=0x04000004; movs r2,#1`, then the loop `ldrh r1,[r0]; tst r1,r2; beq` (`0x080003A6`-`0x080003AA`) until DISPSTAT's VBlank flag is set; then the VBlank count at `0x0200A870` against `0x02009930`, back to the start while short, and the count zeroed. Found by sampling the PC every 1000 GBA cycles: 48% of a layer's cycles and 57% of a battle's in that 16-byte span. mGBA does not take it for an idle loop: its `GBAIORead` clears `haltPending` on any register read but a constant one, and DISPSTAT is not one (`src/gba/io.c`). The hook (`BN6_AWAIT_FRAME_LOOP`) halts the CPU there while DISPSTAT's VBlank flag (bit 0) is clear and its interrupt is on (DISPSTAT bit 3, IE bit 0, IME bit 0), so the VBlank interrupt ends the halt by the time the flag would end the loop. Verify: the autopilot's debug log and run log are the same with and without it, and `CYBERWORLD_EMU_DEBUG`'s 30-frame line counts about four more hook hits a frame (each interrupt that wakes the CPU before VBlank brings it back to the loop). |
