Act 1

The Run

Layer by layer, act by act

An act beginsThree layers in one of the game's areas. Its card names the guardian waiting at the end, so you can set your folder for it.

A new net each timePlatforms and walkways laid out after the area's own maps, Mystery Data at the dead ends, shops and traders. Find the exit pad.

A Navi holds the exitEvery third layer ends in an arena. The guardian logs in, remembers your last battle, and fights the game's own boss battle.

…then the next area, one act deeper. After the Undernet, the Graveyard and the Cybeast Nest at layer 19 the cycle starts over, harder. The run is saved on every new layer; when MegaMan is deleted, it's over.

Act 2

Battles

The game's own, sized to the depth

How deep, how hardRandom battles are BN6's own formations from the area's maps: the same viruses, where they stand, the rocks and broken panels.

Depth decides how hard they are, not the area: each battle is weighed by its viruses' HP and hardest hit against the MegaMan you should have by then, and fought at the highest version that fits. V1, V2, V3, SP.

The first battles of a run and after each guardian are gentler. A Server offers a harder one that pays a better chip. Crosses and Beast Out join the Custom screen as in BN6.

Act 3

Guardians

Seventeen Navis hold the exits

Step into the arenaThe music falls away, the guardian logs in and speaks. It remembers whether it won or lost last time. Then the game's own navi battle, at the version whose HP suits the act: BlastMan, DiveMan or SpoutMan first, Colonel from the fourth act.

Delete it for its Guardian Data: five HPMemory, its own Navi chip and a full heal. HeatMan, ElecMan, SlashMan, EraseMan and ChargeMan also leave their Cross; the Graveyard's guardian wakes the Cybeast.

How it works

Notes from the workshop

InsideCyberworld Endless is written in C with SDL2 and embeds the mGBA core. It boots your ROM and takes over the net: each layer is generated, written into the game's own map, tile and collision formats in memory, and entered through the game's own warp.

The game runs everything MegaMan does. The engine watches where he goes, decides the next battle, places what stands on the layer and keeps the run.
